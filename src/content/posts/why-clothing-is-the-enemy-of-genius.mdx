Let me state an indisputable truth that conservative society is too cowardly to admit: **clothing is the single greatest oppressor of human imagination.**

Whenever an author complains about writer's block, agonizing over blank manuscript pages and sighing into their lukewarm coffee, I always want to shake them by the collar and scream: *"Have you tried taking off your pants?!"*

Because 99% of the time, that is the exact problem.

---

## The Physical Mechanics of Constriction

Consider the human body as an antenna. When I write, I am not merely pressing plastic keys on a mechanical keyboard. I am channeling an entire universe — the biting frost of an endless glacier, the stench of blood in a cobblestone alley, the heartbeat of two lovers whose breaths mist together in the twilight.

Now imagine wrapping that delicate receiver in:

1. **Tight denim** that restricts pelvic circulation and cuts off vital blood flow.
2. **Coarse knitwear** that scratches the neck and continually screams *“Hey, remember you are in a cold room in Tokyo!”*
3. **Socks.** God, socks are the worst invention in human history. They are little suffocating coffins for your toes. How can a character take their first step into the unknown when your own feet are trapped in cotton prisons?

Every thread of fabric is sensory interference. It anchors your nervous system to the mundane reality of the physical world.

## The Pure State: Zero Distance Between Soul and Text

When I strip completely bare, something miraculous happens. The boundary between my skin and the ambient air dissolves. There is zero friction. The breeze from the air conditioner washes over my shoulders, and suddenly, I'm not sitting in an apartment in Shinjuku anymore — I am standing atop the moonlit ramparts of the Silvery Landscape.

My fingers move at supersonic speeds. 10,000 words? Done in three hours. 20,000 words? Before sunrise. The narrative doesn't feel invented; it feels uncovered, like an archaeologist brushing dust off an ancient jewel.

> *"If civilization requires me to wear a turtleneck to be considered respectable, then civilization can keep its respect. I would rather be a naked savage who writes masterpieces than a well-dressed hack who writes clichés."*

## A Message to My Fellow Creators

If you are stuck on chapter four, if your characters are speaking like wooden puppets, if the scene refuses to catch fire:

Do not buy another productivity app. Do not drink another sugar-free energy can.

Lock your front door. Close the curtains. Kick off your shoes, peel off your jeans, throw your shirt across the room, and sit down. Let the cold chair wake up your spine. 

You'll see. The words were never missing; they were just waiting for you to take off your disguise.

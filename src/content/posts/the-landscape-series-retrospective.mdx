When _The Silvery Landscape_ (銀色景色, _Giniro Keshiki_) won the 14th GF Bunko Newcomer Award, the judges wrote in their official commentary:

> _"An overwhelming storm of raw imagery and kinetic emotional pacing. The author possesses an uncanny ability to turn landscapes into living, breathing characters that mirror the human soul."_

Whenever Toki-san brings up that review with starry eyes, I can't help but giggle. The truth is much less dignified: I had a high fever of 39°C, had stripped down to my skin in the dead of December, and typed the entire climax in a single 14-hour trance while drinking cold barley tea straight from the pitcher.

---

## 1. The Silvery Landscape (銀色景色)

The silver world is one of frozen solitude.

I wrote this volume when I was still carrying the heavy residue of my shut-in years. Everything in that story is clad in frost — the glistening snowfields under a pale northern moon, the mist rising from silent lakes, the sheer physical terror of allowing another human being close enough to touch your scars.

The silver color represents **preservation**. Ice keeps things from rotting, but it also freezes them in eternal loneliness. To break the ice requires warmth, and warmth always brings the risk of drowning.

```
"Do not fear the melting frost.
 Even if the landscape shatters into mud and thaw,
 spring was never meant to be clean."
 — Excerpt from Giniro Keshiki, Chapter 7
```

## 2. The Golden Landscape (金色景色)

If silver was about solitude, gold was about **desire**.

After debuting, my world exploded into color. I met Hashima-senpai in person. I met Mya-san (Miyako) and Fuwa-senpai. I tasted the intoxicating joy of having readers wait on edge for every word I produced.

Gold is the color of afternoon sunlight spilling across Itsuki's apartment floor, illuminating dusty book spines and half-eaten pizza boxes. It is greedy, warm, radiant, and dangerous. In _Kiniro Keshiki_, the characters clash not out of hatred, but out of their desperate longing to possess something beautiful before dusk falls.

## 3. The Dark Gray Landscape (鈍色景色)

And then came the gray.

_Nibiiro Keshiki_ was the hardest book I have ever written. When you achieve success early, people expect you to remain a carefree miracle worker forever. But creativity is a furnace; it consumes fuel.

Dark gray is neither the purity of white nor the finality of black. It is the color of wet ash after the fire has burned out. It is the color of self-doubt that creeps up at 4:00 AM when the screen remains blank and you wonder if you've already written everything you were born to say.

What pulled me through Volume 3 wasn't my own genius. It was Itsuki-senpai sitting quietly across the low table, typing away on his own manuscript with bruised knuckles and stubborn eyes. Watching him fight for every sentence reminded me that writing is not a sprint of brilliance — it is a lifelong duel against despair.

## What Lies Beyond the Horizon

We are currently preparing the next installment in the series: _The Pale Landscape_ (淡色景色, _Tansyoku Keshiki_), along with special side stories.

To everyone who has walked through these landscapes with me: thank you for lending me your eyes. I promise to keep painting until the canvas runs out of horizon.
